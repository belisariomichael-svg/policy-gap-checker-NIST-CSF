Controls (NIST CSF 2.0 categories):
GV.OC | Organizational Context: Mission, stakeholder expectations, and legal/regulatory requirements that shape cybersecurity risk decisions are understood.
GV.RM | Risk Management Strategy: Risk appetite, tolerance, and the strategy for managing cybersecurity risk are established and communicated.
GV.RR | Roles, Responsibilities, and Authorities: Cybersecurity roles, responsibilities, and authorities are established, communicated, and enforced.
GV.PO | Policy: Cybersecurity policy is established, communicated, enforced, and reviewed on a defined cycle.
GV.OV | Oversight: Results of cybersecurity risk management are reviewed to inform and adjust strategy.
GV.SC | Cybersecurity Supply Chain Risk Management: Supply chain and third-party risks are identified, assessed, and managed.
ID.AM | Asset Management: Assets (hardware, software, data, services) are inventoried and managed by importance to objectives.
ID.RA | Risk Assessment: Cybersecurity risk to the organization, assets, and individuals is understood through threat, vulnerability, and impact analysis.
ID.IM | Improvement: Improvements to cybersecurity risk management are identified from evaluations, tests, and lessons learned.
PR.AA | Identity Management, Authentication, and Access Control: Access to assets is limited to authorized users, services, and hardware and managed by risk.
PR.AT | Awareness and Training: Personnel are given cybersecurity awareness and role-based training.
PR.DS | Data Security: Data is managed to protect confidentiality, integrity, and availability (at rest, in transit, in use).
PR.PS | Platform Security: Hardware, software, and services are managed per risk (configuration, patching, logging, secure development).
PR.IR | Technology Infrastructure Resilience: Security architectures are managed to protect asset confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience.
DE.CM | Continuous Monitoring: Assets are monitored to find anomalies, indicators of compromise, and other adverse events.
DE.AE | Adverse Event Analysis: Anomalies and potential adverse events are analyzed to characterize them and detect incidents.
RS.MA | Incident Management: Responses to detected incidents are managed (triage, escalation, declaration).
RS.AN | Incident Analysis: Investigations support effective response and recovery, including forensics and root cause.
RS.CO | Incident Response Reporting and Communication: Response activities are coordinated with internal and external stakeholders.
RS.MI | Incident Mitigation: Activities are performed to prevent expansion of an event and mitigate its effects.
RC.RP | Incident Recovery Plan Execution: Restoration activities are performed to ensure operational availability of affected systems.
RC.CO | Incident Recovery Communication: Recovery activities and progress are communicated to stakeholders.

<policy>
Information Security Policy (Sample, v1.2)

1. Purpose. This policy sets out the company's approach to protecting information assets and supports our regulatory obligations.

2. Roles. The Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) is accountable for this policy. Department heads are responsible for compliance within their teams.

3. Access control. Access to systems is granted on a least-privilege basis and requires approval from the line manager. Multi-factor authentication is required for all remote access. User access rights are reviewed every six months.

4. Training. All employees must complete security awareness training when they join and once a year.

5. Data protection. Confidential data must be encrypted in transit. Removable media should be avoided where possible.

6. Incident reporting. Employees must report suspected security incidents to the IT service desk immediately.

7. Vendors. Third parties with access to company data must sign a confidentiality agreement.

8. Review. This policy is reviewed annually by the CISO.
</policy>
